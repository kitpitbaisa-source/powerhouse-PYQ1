# Project Rules & Agent Instructions

## Database Synchronization
- All questions are primarily stored in `src/questions.ts` as `mcqData`.
- The backend (`server.ts`) is configured to automatically sync `mcqData` to Firestore whenever the total count in Firestore is less than the count in `src/questions.ts`.
- **Constraint**: Whenever the user provides a new question, you MUST add it to `src/questions.ts` with a unique, incrementing `id`. 
- After adding questions to `src/questions.ts`, the deployment/restart process will automatically push them to the live database.

## Subscription Access
- Users not in the `users` Firestore collection or marked as `not_subscribed` are limited to questions from the latest two years.
- Other questions will show a "Premium Question" lock template with instructions to contact the admin.

## Concept Extraction and Highlight Evidence
- Extract `highlight_evidence` alongside concepts for every new question profile, including agent-authored imports. Do not defer highlight extraction until similarity matching.
- Review the question stem, every option, and the supplied explanation for important phrases that name or describe each materially tested primary or supporting concept. Include materially relevant contrasts and distractors, not incidental words or answer-selection codes.
- Link each excerpt to its assigned `concept_id` and source `field`; option excerpts also require a zero-based `option_index`. Copy `exact_text` verbatim, preserving spelling, case, punctuation, and spacing. Keep excerpts short and renderable without spanning HTML tags.
- Reuse the same canonical concept ID across questions only when meaning and scope match. Preserve each question's own exact wording for highlights; do not force identical highlight text into differently worded questions.
- Include important source evidence even when a concept is not shared with another question. Do not restrict evidence to the correct option, a shared concept, or a canonical-name match.
- Use the shortest complete meaningful phrase, retaining essential numbers, dates, units, qualifiers and negation. Statements are part of `field: "question"`; review evidence may be a full sentence, but display evidence must not copy a whole sentence or paragraph when a shorter phrase preserves the tested meaning.
- New processing requires `concept_review.highlights` with `status`, `rationale`, `gaps`, `extended_excerpts`, and `dense_fields`. Review the stem, every option and supplied explanation through the existing material-item coverage inventory. Each concept-bearing source passage in that inventory needs a corresponding concise highlight or a gap identifying its `item_id`, `concept_id`, `field`, optional `option_index`, and specific `reason`. A gap must reflect genuinely implicit or non-renderable evidence, not avoidable extraction work; never invent evidence.
- For any source field with no highlights and no material-item gap, record a specific `highlight_gap` in its `concept_review.coverage.reviewed_fields` entry. Explain why it has no suitable concept phrase (for example, an instruction-only stem or generic answer-code option). Evidence in an option or explanation must not silently substitute for an available stem phrase, or vice versa.
- Excerpts over 24 words or 180 characters require an exact evidence entry and reason in `extended_excerpts`. Highlighting over 70% of a source field containing at least 40 words requires a field-specific reason in `dense_fields`. These are review triggers, not uniform density/count targets or automatic truncation limits. Keep all materially necessary phrases and justify genuine exceptions; do not remove units or conditions merely to pass a threshold.
- Bind the final highlight review to the same source/proposal/registry audit as the concept review. Missing, uncertain, or incomplete highlight coverage must hold new processing with `HIGHLIGHT_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, before concept writes or embedding. Legacy stored profiles remain readable; these rules do not authorize bulk regeneration of saved highlights.
- Leave generic statement-code options unhighlighted when they contain no substantive concept phrase. Highlighting indicates a concept mention, not that an option is true or correct.

## Concept Granularity and Cross-Question Consistency
- When processing a subject, use the active `subject_topic_master` as the authority for subject/topic/subtopic IDs, names, and placement. Reuse equivalent active subtopics and consult legacy topic `details` before proposing anything new. New subtopics require explicit approval before creation and are separate `type: "subtopic"` records with stable IDs such as `S12-T01-ST01`, the active topic ID as `parentId`, and the subject ID as partition key. Create approved entries only for distinct, source-supported areas not already covered. Preserve existing subject/topic records, legacy `details`, IDs, and unrelated mappings; never renumber or reuse IDs. Extractions carry the exact subtopic names and matching IDs in `classification.master`. Back up master changes, use create-only writes for new IDs and optimistic concurrency for approved updates, and confirm writes by reading them back. Uncertain placement goes to `pending_review` rather than creating a speculative subtopic.
- Subtopics are classification, card-display and filtering metadata only for now. Save exact approved names in `classification.subtopics` and their same-order IDs in `classification.master.subtopicIds`. Do not add these names, IDs, paths or the full classification/master JSON to embedding inputs. Subtopic-only changes must leave `buildEmbeddingInput` text and its embedding-input hash unchanged. Do not copy category labels into concept summaries, underlying principles or knowledge requirements merely to make them affect embeddings; retain specific, source-supported concepts even when their wording happens to match a subtopic name. Changing this embedding policy requires separate explicit approval.
- If a question requires a subtopic not represented by an equivalent active master subtopic, flag it instead of automatically adding a master record, inventing an ID, or forcing an unrelated mapping. Record `NEW_SUBTOPIC_REVIEW_REQUIRED` against the `master_classification` check, with the proposed subtopic name, proposed existing parent subject/topic IDs, literal source evidence, and required approval in the issue and review notes. Save the source/profile review flags using the rules below, keep the profile `pending_review` with `embedding: null`, continue the remaining questions, and include affected question IDs and proposed subtopics in the batch report.
- Extract each independently tested fact, association, mechanism, or principle at a consistent, reusable scope. A statement or matching question must retain the concepts of its individual items; a broad topic or umbrella concept must not replace them.
- Do not cap a question at one concept. Include all distinct, materially tested concepts with their own source evidence and reviewed roles, including supporting principles needed for the solution. Keep a single concept when the question tests only one fact; do not split synonyms into duplicate concepts or add incidental concepts to manufacture overlap.
- Check the canonical registry and related existing profiles before proposing a new ID. Reuse the same ID when a tested association appears as a standalone question, one matching pair, or one statement. For example, Senguttuvan's Chera affiliation must use the same concept in an identity question and a multi-ruler matching question.
- Preserve distinct associations within compound questions, including correctly matched pairs and incorrect claims evaluated using the supplied explanation. Keep claimed and source-supported relationships separate; sharing a concept does not endorse a false claim.
- Keep a useful umbrella concept as supporting context where appropriate, alongside the specific tested concepts. Do not merge broad and narrow concepts as aliases or assume a parent link automatically produces a shared-concept label: matching currently uses exact assigned `concept_id` overlap.
- Do not add a concept merely to increase similarity. Reuse requires materially tested meaning and scope, not just a shared entity, incidental explanation detail, or high embedding similarity.
- Bind each claim and highlight to its specific assigned concepts. Before saving, verify that repeated facts use consistent IDs, all materially tested items remain represented, and each highlight is an exact excerpt from its own question, option, or explanation.
- When changing concept assignments, validate the complete source-bound extraction and regenerate the affected embedding through the import pipeline. Do not patch only a displayed label, canonical name, or stored vector.
- Concept identity is independent of role: reuse the same canonical ID when a fact or principle is primary in one question and secondary, prerequisite, or application in another.
- Primary concepts are directly tested; secondary concepts materially support solving the question. Lower cosine similarity, loose topical relatedness, or incidental mentions do not justify secondary assignments.
- Before concept creation or embedding, obtain a source-grounded meaning/scope and role review for every assignment. Reuse equivalent registry entries even when names differ; create only clearly distinct concepts. Explain both identity and role decisions with literal source evidence.
- Before accepting any proposed new concept, perform a final semantic identity review against the current live `concepts` registry and all proposed concepts across every worker in the batch. Compare the tested fact, association, mechanism or principle and its scope using source questions and explanations, including synonyms and paraphrased definitions. Exact ID, name, alias or definition matching alone is insufficient; neither a worker's initial registry snapshot nor a high embedding score proves novelty or equivalence.
- Reconcile equivalent cross-worker proposals to one canonical ID before any concept write or embedding. For example, questions asking for NISAR's NASA partner and its joint NASA-ISRO developers test the same partnership identity despite differently worded concept names. Preserve each question's reviewed roles and literal evidence when remapping all concept references; validate and reimport the complete affected extraction and regenerate its embedding. Do not merge broad, narrow or merely related concepts.
- Record the final reviewer, compared candidate IDs, source evidence and rationale for each reuse/new/review decision. Refresh the registry before persistence and re-review affected decisions if concurrent writes change the candidates; a unique ID or create-only write does not prevent duplicate meaning under different IDs. Block new concept creation and embeddings until this review is complete. Uncertain identity goes to `pending_review` with a specific reason and required action. These checks concern `concepts`; they do not authorize changes to `subject_topic_master`.
- Uncertain equivalence, unsupported roles, and missing reviews go to `pending_review` without creating concepts or publishing a vector. Continue other batch entries and report pending-review question IDs at the end.
- Uncertain, conflicting, or insufficiently supported subject/topic classifications also go to `pending_review`; do not abort the remaining imports or correct source labels until the classification is supported.
- An explicitly authorized independent question classification records `classification.review.taxonomy_policy: "independent_source"` and an active-master mapping. Source-supported question labels may then differ from a shared concept's legacy subject/topic labels without changing its ID or meaning. This opt-in does not waive literal primary evidence, confidence, active-master validation, complete new-extraction meaning/role/coverage/highlight admission, global identity reconciliation, or source-quality flags. Normal classifications retain canonical-anchor matching. Maintenance records the actual legacy-label mismatch separately; never alter canonical metadata merely to satisfy question-label equality.
- Offline imports must supply the same `concept_review` decisions; do not introduce text-generation calls into the embedding-only import route. Do not bulk merge existing concepts by cosine score.
- New pipeline admissions require `concept_review.coverage` for every source field, material item, assignment and claim, plus explicit `compared_concept_ids`. An offline batch's identified final reviewer must reconcile all workers' proposals and bind the live registry and complete entries in `registry_review`; fingerprint helpers record that review, not perform it. Shared new meanings use one final ID and identical definition across entries before any write. Single-entry imports need a source/proposal/registry-bound `concept_review.audit`. Missing or stale review is a durable hold, not permission to create.
- Explicitly approved classification/embedding-only maintenance uses `ConceptPipeline.maintainEmbedding`, not a relaxed normal import. It may preserve an existing complete concept profile and highlights without a fresh full review only when the original source hash is proven, source content other than subject/topic is unchanged, the new classification has literal primary evidence and exact active-master mappings, and every existing canonical reference remains valid. Unresolved non-classification flags, uncertain existing reviews and source conflicts stay blocked; classification flags may clear only after those supported classification checks pass. Do not create concepts, alter roles or rewrite concept text in this path. Compare the final embedding input and reuse an unchanged compatible vector; generate missing, changed-input or incompatible vectors. Keep verification separate and record the limited scope in `processing.maintenance`, including that no fresh concept/highlight review was performed.
- Explicitly authorized source-only classification correction uses `ConceptPipeline.maintainClassification` with literal tested question/option evidence, exact active master mappings, backed-up current source/profile bindings and optimistic concurrency. An incomplete concept extraction does not prevent classification review. This path never invents a primary concept anchor, performs concept/highlight admission, or generates embeddings: preserve protected profile content and unrelated holds, keep the profile pending review with a null vector, and record the limited classification review in `processing.classificationMaintenance`. Missing master scope still requires explicit approval rather than a forced child.
- Explicitly authorized parent-only repair uses `maintainClassificationParents` only when the active subject/topic are source-supported but a classification issue remains unresolved. Require current source/profile ETags, literal tested evidence and proven source binding; retain every issue and its approval requirement, mirror pending checks, clear unapproved child references to empty arrays, keep a null vector, and audit `processing.classificationParentMaintenance`. This does not mark classification passed or relax full admission. Ambiguous parents remain held.
- Canonical writes must go through the pipeline's Cosmos-backed registry coordinator. Do not bypass it with direct SDK writes, auto-expire its lock, or release another live worker. A retained lock after a crash or uncertain write requires stopping the owner, settling and reconciling its recorded proposal, and explicit owner-bound recovery; it must never become a duplicate-creation fallback.
- Approved equivalent-ID consolidations retain the old canonical record as retired with replacement/reviewer/audit metadata; do not overwrite either meaning or delete history. Back up and fully review every dependent profile, preflight the exact post-retirement registry and complete imports, then stage retirement and import all remapped assignments/claims/highlights under the same owned coordinator. Unreviewed dependencies, parent links, active leases, uncertain writes or incomplete migrations must block completion and retain the owner-bound journal for reconciliation.
- Embeddings must use the Gemini API (`models.embedContent` with the configured `embeddingModel`, `embeddingPrefix` and dimensions from `features/similarity/config`) through the pipeline's `embed` call, authenticated with the project's own `.env` key. Never use another embedding provider, mix vectors from different models, or write vectors directly. Any model, prefix or dimension change requires re-embedding every profile through the pipeline. Never print API keys.
- Concept and classification extraction or repair performed in maintenance sessions (for example filling `question_format`, `polarity`, `reasoning_type` and `question_intent` on held profiles) must use Copilot local: read the source question, write the values by hand from its literal text, prefer vocabulary already used by `ready` profiles, save them for final-reviewer approval before any write, and never call the Gemini extraction model for them. Gemini remains the embedding provider only.

## Consistent Review Flags for All Questions
- Apply the same review-flag convention to every subject, exam, source container and import method, not just Geography questions.
- Store canonical `verification` metadata on the source question itself in every bank and mirror it in the shared question profile. Use `verification.status` consistently: `unverified`, `verified`, or `pending_review`. Missing legacy metadata means unverified, never verified. `processing.status: "ready"` means only that the embedding pipeline completed.
- Keep separate statuses, reasons and evidence in `verification.checks.answer`, `.explanation`, `.master_classification` (subject/topic), `.question`, and `.concepts`. Each check uses `not_checked`, `passed`, or `pending_review`. Derive `verification.reason` and blocked-check reasons from the structured issues; do not maintain contradictory duplicate flags.
- Patch only `/verification` on source documents with content-hash checks, optimistic concurrency, and read-back verification. Exclude this bookkeeping metadata from the normalized source hash. Source review blocks must prevent matching even when an older profile still says ready.
- Mark `verified` only after the question, answer, explanation, active master classification, and concepts all have passed evidence-backed checks. Record `reviewedBy`, `updatedAt`, source and reviewed-extraction hashes, and the master subject/topic IDs. Agent agreement with the supplied answer/explanation is not independent factual verification.
- Record corrections in `verification.corrections` with the field, reason, and an audit reference to the backed-up source change. A correct question can be verified without needing a correction.
- Keep blocking findings in `verification.issues` with a stable code, affected check, specific reason, available evidence, and required action. Keep these synchronized with the existing pending-review status, provenance, null vector, and `REVIEW_FLAG[...]` error below.
- Do not clear an existing blocking issue through `--force`, source edits, or an unchecked reimport. Require an identified reviewer and evidence-backed passed checks for the issues being resolved. Source or extraction changes invalidate previous full verification.
- When an issue cannot be safely resolved, save the question profile with `processing.status: "pending_review"`, `provenance.needs_review: true` and `embedding: null`. Do not guess an answer, invent original exam wording, force a classification or create uncertain concepts.
- Set `processing.error` to `REVIEW_FLAG[ISSUE_CODE]: specific reason`, using a stable, descriptive issue code. In `provenance.review_notes`, record the exact issue, available source evidence or conflicting claims, relevant references when available, and the action or evidence required to clear the flag later.
- Preserve unresolved source wording, options and answer keys; do not silently rewrite them to make an invalid or ambiguous item appear valid. Back up any verified source correction and keep the profile bound to the resulting source content hash.
- Save flags durably, confirm each new write by reading it back, and continue other questions instead of blocking the batch on exhaustive research. Include flagged question IDs, reasons and required follow-up in the review report.
- Keep unresolved flagged questions out of accepted embedding and retry queues. A source-reported dropped question must not be assigned an invented official scoring option.
- Explicitly approved source-quality caveats may instead be retained as nonblocking `verification.warnings` for `SOURCE_ROUTE_ROUNDING_REVIEW_FLAG` and `CLASS_SIZE_NONINTEGER_REVIEW_FLAG` only. Each warning requires its evidence, an identified reviewer, acknowledgement and recorded per-question user authorization. These questions remain unverified, retain their caveats in provenance and similarity displays, and must pass the complete classification, concept, highlight and embedding admission reviews. This does not relax any other blocking issue or authorize automatic conversion of existing holds. A source-content change invalidates the acknowledgement and restores the warning as a blocking issue until reviewed again.
- Clear a blocking flag only after its stated issue is resolved with source-grounded evidence and the complete affected extraction is validated. Record the resolution and update any dependent review metadata consistently; do not clear advisory provenance warnings merely to make a record look complete.
